\subsection*{Getting Started}
    Pick some dice, a Referee and make them do a game for you.

    Create a knight, pick gear and go for it.

\subsection*{Scope}
    From one-shots to long adventures, but probably not a full campaign.

\subsection*{Dice Rolls}

To resolve some events a roll is needed. 
There are two mechanics that modify roll itself or dice in the roll. 

\paragraph{Upface \& Downface.}

Certain abilities can \texttt{Upface} and \texttt{Downface} a die. 
To \texttt{Upface} a die, find it in the sequence:

\colbox{
    \[\texttt{d1, d4, d6, d8, d12}\]
}

And replace it with the next one. To \texttt{Downface} a die, replace it with previous one. 
\texttt{d1} can't be \texttt{Downfaced} and \texttt{d12} can't be \texttt{Upfaced}. 

You probably don't have a \texttt{d1} in your possession, but as it always rolls \texttt{1}, you can use any small object to represent it.

When you are \texttt{Upfacing} or \texttt{Downfacing} a die in an \texttt{Attack} roll do that before declaring \texttt{Focus} usage.


\paragraph{Highroll \& Lowroll.}

There are several types of rolls: attack roll, virtue check, spellcasting roll and more. 
Many of them, including named three, can be \texttt{Highrolled} and \texttt{Lowrolled}. 

To \texttt{Highroll} a roll, add a duplicate of a die that already exists in the roll. 
Perform a roll and then remove a die from it which shares amount of faces with the one added. 
Typically, you want to remove the lowest one, but ultimately its for you to decide which one to remove.

It is possible to double \texttt{Highroll} by adding two dice before the roll and then removing two that share amount of faces with those added. 
Naturally, this approach extends to any degree of \texttt{Highrollness}. 

To \texttt{Lowroll} -- do the same, but you must remove the highest dice for each degree of Lowrollness.

If there is a need to simultaneously \texttt{Highroll} and \texttt{Lowroll} remove \texttt{High-} and \texttt{Low-} pairs until only one type of "-rollness" remains.

It is possible to \texttt{Highroll} and \texttt{Lowroll} after the actual roll is performed. 
To do that choose a die that is present in the roll and roll it in addition to those already there. 
Then proceed as if they were rolled together.

\subsection*{Virtues}

Each knight has three attributes that describe his body, mind and soul. 


\colbox{ 
\textbf{Vigor.}
Strong limbs, firm hands, powerful lungs.

\textbf{Clarity.}
Keen instinct, lucid mind, shrewd eyes.

\textbf{Spirit.}
Charming tongue, iron will, fierce heart.
}

Knight's creation starts by rolling \texttt{d6 + d12} four times; record not only the sum, but also individual values of each die.
Each of those rolls determines one of the knight's \texttt{Virtues}, you choose which is which and discard the remaining one. 

Then, determine knight's capacity for \texttt{Injury}, \texttt{Focus} and \texttt{Stress} according to steps below.

\colbox{
\begin{itemize}
    \item Half, rounded up, of \texttt{d6} you assigned to \texttt{Vigor} is the amount of \texttt{Lethal Injuries} the knight can survive before dying.
    \item Half, rounded up, of \texttt{d12} you assigned to \texttt{Vigor} is the amount of \texttt{Serious Injuries} the knight can survive before dying.
    \item The value of \texttt{d6} you assigned to \texttt{Clarity} is maximum \texttt{Focus} the knight has.
    \item Half, rounded up, of \texttt{d12} you assigned to \texttt{Spirit} is maximum \texttt{Stress} the knight can endure before suffering a \texttt{Trauma}, minimum is \texttt{2}.
\end{itemize}
}
\textbf{Guard.} Roll \texttt{2d4} to determine the maximum \texttt{Guard} the knight has.

\paragraph{Feats. } Knight knows all three \texttt{Feats}: \texttt{Smite}, \texttt{Focus} and \texttt{Deny}. They can perform them during combat, details of which are described later.

\subsection*{Weapons \& Gear}

Weapons have properties to reflect their behavior.

\begin{itemize}[nosep]
    \item \textbf{Hefty} weapons require one hand. Only one \textbf{Hefty} item can be wielded at once.
    \item \textbf{Long} weapons require both hands and are \texttt{Impaired} in confined environments.
    \item \textbf{Slow} weapons cannot be used if the attacker has moved this turn.
\end{itemize}
\textbf{Common Weapons.} 
\begin{itemize}[nosep, leftmargin=*]
    \item [] Hefty, simple tools. \texttt{d6 hefty}: Pitchfork, Hatchet
    \item [] Long, Slow Tools. \texttt{d8 long, slow}: staff, logging axe, pick
    \item [] Hand Weapons. \texttt{d6}: dagger, club, handaxe
    \item [] Hefty Weapons. \texttt{d8 hefty}: spear, mace, axe
    \item [] Long Weapons. \texttt{d10 long}: poleaxe, billhook
    \item [] Sling: \texttt{d4 hefty}
    \item [] Javelin: \texttt{d6 hefty}
\end{itemize}
\textbf{Uncommon Weapons.} 
\begin{itemize}[nosep, leftmargin=*]
    \item [] Shortbow: \texttt{d6 long, slow}
    \item [] Shortsword: \texttt{2d6}
    \item [] Longbow: \texttt{d8 slow}
\end{itemize}
\textbf{Rare Weapons.} 
\begin{itemize}[nosep, leftmargin=*]
    \item [] Longsword: \texttt{2d8 hefty}
    \item [] Greatsword: \texttt{2d10 long, slow}
    \item [] Curvebow: \texttt{2d6 long, slow}
    \item [] Crossbow: \texttt{2d8 slow}
\end{itemize}
\textbf{Armour.} Generally, each layer of armour gives an additional armour point. 
Steel protection is rare, so helms, plates and chainmail are very valuable assets. 
You rather likely to find:
\begin{itemize}[nosep, leftmargin=*]
    \item[] Coat: A1 (mail, gambeson, flexible armour suitable for general wear or beneath plates)
    \item[] Helm: A1 (kettle, nasal, bucket, coif)
\end{itemize} 
\textbf{Shields.} Takes one hand, gives one point in armour, common to find. 
Does not stack if there are two shield. It has \texttt{d4} as a weapon die.

Spiked shields turn that die into \texttt{d6}.


\paragraph{Magic} can be found on Kerkenna and, though not frequently, knights known to employ it during their endeavours. Some knights are \textbf{Mages} and some are \textbf{Witches}.

\subsection*{Focus \& Stress}

There are two important resources each knight has: 
the amount of thought the knight can put into perfecting a task and the amount of effort the can put into a failed task to fix it.
Those are tracked by \texttt{Focus} and \texttt{Stress} respectively.

\paragraph{Focus.} When a roll is performed that can be effected by a character, they can spend Focus to 
enhance it. It \textbf{must} be declared \textbf{before} rolling the dice. For each point of \texttt{Focus} spent -- \texttt{Highroll} that roll.

One can not only plot how to enhance the roll, but also how to sabotage a roll. 
If one character can affect the roll of another in a destructive way, for each \texttt{Focus} spent the roll is \texttt{Lowrolled}.

Usually, a knight has no restriction how much \texttt{Focus} they can spend to enhance an action, but
determining how much focus is possible to spend to oppose a given action should be discussed with Referee.

The order is important, so opposing characters decide how much \texttt{Focus} they spend simultaneously.

\paragraph{Stress.} Increasing one's \texttt{Stress} is done similarly, but \textbf{must} be done \textbf{after} the roll.

After a roll is made, a knight can decide that they are not satisfied with the result and try to fix it by acquiring \texttt{Stress}. 
For each gained \texttt{Stress}, \texttt{Highroll} that roll.

One can also acquire \texttt{Stress} to oppose an action after the roll is made. 

Opposing characters decide how much \texttt{Stress} they spend simultaneously.

Character can't spend \texttt{Stress} if they are at maximum stress. 

After resolving an action, check if your stress is at maximum. 
If it is, set it to zero and the character suffers a \texttt{Trauma}(see \texttt{Injuries} section for details).

\newcolumn

\paragraph{Saves.}

To pass a Save, roll a \texttt{d20} equal or below the
relevant \texttt{Virtue}. Failure means negative
consequences, not always a failed action.

\paragraph{Turns.} A turn is enough time to Move and then
perform an Action, always in that order.

\subsection*{Attacks}

Take the \texttt{Weapon Dice} noted on your weapon(s), shield, and granted by bonuses.
Go through the following steps in order:

\begin{enumerate}[nosep, leftmargin=*]
    \item Declare and spend \texttt{Focus}, target declares opposition and matching \texttt{High-} and \texttt{Lowrolls} are removed.
    \item All combatants that attack the same target roll simultaneously, but do not mix the dice together as \texttt{Focus} and \texttt{Stress} is applied separately.
    \item Remove extra dice added through \texttt{Focus}.
    \item Declare \texttt{Stress} gain, target declares opposition, remove matching \texttt{High-} and \texttt{Lowrolls} roll additional dice.
    \item Remove extra dice from \texttt{Stress}.
    \item Now you can mix all dice together and declare which one is used as a damage die and which are spent on \texttt{Gambits}.
    \item If the target or their nearby allies can \texttt{Deny} they may use it.
    \item If the damage die is discarded - assign a new one, but \texttt{Gambit} allocations can't change. If no die remains, assign \texttt{d1}.
    \item Add any extra Damage from attackers who are \texttt{Bolstering} the Attack.
    \item Subtract the target’s total \texttt{Armour score}.
    \item The Attack causes that much \texttt{Combat Damage}.
\end{enumerate}

\paragraph{Imaired} attacks roll d4 only and cannot
gain bonus dice or benefit from Feats.

\paragraph{Blast} attacks target everybody in their area,
rolling each separately.

\end{multicols*}

\begin{multicols*}{2}

\subsection*{Combat Damage.}

The attack’s Damage is split between the
target’s \texttt{Guard} and their \texttt{Vigor} at target's discretion, both are reduced by respective amount.

If there was no overspill into \texttt{Vigor} then they evade the \texttt{Attack}.

If it overspills into target’s \texttt{Vigor} -- they are Wounded.

Check if damage to \texttt{Vigor} causes a \texttt{Scar} or an \texttt{Injury}. 

\subsection*{Virtue loss.}

Victims are only Slain, Mortally Wounded or Injured if the Virtue was reduced by \texttt{Combat Damage}, never as a result of Virtue Loss.

At \texttt{0} \texttt{Vigor} you are \texttt{Exhausted} and cannot Attack if you have moved this turn.

At \texttt{0} \texttt{Clarity} you are Exposed and treated as having \texttt{0} \texttt{Guard}.

At \texttt{0} \texttt{Spirit} your attacks are Impaired rolling a single d4 only.

\paragraph{Death.} Even the boldest Knight can die a sudden or ignoble death. Prepare yourself for this.
When a Knight dies the player creates a new Knight and they are added to the Company as quickly as possible. 

\subsection*{Feats}

These special actions known by knights can
be performed as described below. Using Feats
risks the knight becoming \texttt{Fatigued} which
prevents them from using further \texttt{Feats} until
they rest.
\textbf{Smite.} \textit{Release your righteous fury} 
\begin{itemize}[nosep]
    \item Use before rolling a melee \texttt{Attack}.
    \item The \texttt{Attack} gains either \texttt{+1d12} or \texttt{Blast}.
    \item Pass \texttt{Vigor} check or become \texttt{Fatigued}.
\end{itemize}
\textbf{Focus.} \textit{Create an opening to exploit} 
\begin{itemize}[nosep]
    \item Use after rolling an \texttt{Attack}.
    \item Perform a Gambit without using a die.
    \item Pass \texttt{Clarity} check or become \texttt{Fatigued}.
\end{itemize}
\textbf{Deny.} \textit{Rebuff an attack before it lands} 
\begin{itemize}[nosep]
    \item Use after an \texttt{Attack} roll against you or an ally within arm’s reach.
    \item Discard one Attack die from the roll.
    \item Pass \texttt{Spirit} check or become \texttt{Fatigued}.
\end{itemize}


\subsection*{Gambits}

Attackers can discard any number of dice in \texttt{Attack Roll} with value 4 or higher to perform \texttt{Gambits}, each one causing one of the effects below.
The affected foe must be the same as the target of the attack, except for \texttt{Patch a Wound} \texttt{Gambit}.

For gambits other then \texttt{Bolster}, \texttt{Move} and \texttt{Patch a Wound} the target receives a \texttt{Vigor Save} to ignore the effect.

\begin{itemize}[nosep]
    \item \textbf{Bolster} the Attack for +1 total Damage
    \item \textbf{Move} after the Attack, even if you already moved or are unable to move
    \item \textbf{Repel} a foe away from you
    \item \textbf{Stop} a foe from moving next turn
    \item \textbf{Impair} a weapon on their next turn
    \item \textbf{Trap} a shield until your next turn
    \item \textbf{Dismount} a foe
    \item \textbf{Patch a Wound} of yourself or an ally at arms range
    \item \textbf{Other effect} of a similar level of impact
\end{itemize}

If a die of \texttt{8} or higher is used in melee then a Strong Gambit is performed and the attacker chooses one of the following:

\begin{itemize}[nosep]
    \item \textbf{No Save} is granted to the target
    \item \textbf{Greater effect} such as disarming an item, breaking a wooden shield or removing a helm. Bolster can't add more than \texttt{1} damage. 
\end{itemize}

\textbf{Patching a Wound} \texttt{Gambit} allows one knight to tend to another one, or one knight to tend to themselves. 
Tending knight rolls a \texttt{Clarity} check and, if they pass, the one untended \texttt{Injury} becomes tended and its consequences disappear.
If the save fails, the \texttt{Injury} is still tended, but the target knight make \texttt{Vigor} save. 
If this one also fails the injury increases in lethality, potentially killing a knight.`'

\subsection*{Injuries} 

A body of a knight is tough and trained since childhood, but it can endure only so much. 
Dice that determine knight's \texttt{Vigor} also defines amount of \texttt{Serious and Lethal Injuries} they can sustain. 

To track those injuries use \texttt{Injury Slots} that record nature of the \texttt{Injury}, damage die that caused it and a \texttt{Consequence} that comes with it.

There are several ways to get an \texttt{Injury} when a knight receives a hit. 

\begin{itemize}[nosep]
    \item[\ding{170}] If a knight lost \texttt{6, 7, 8, or 9} \texttt{Vigor} through combat damage -- take a \texttt{Serious Injury}.
    \item[\textbf{\textdagger}] If a knight lost \texttt{10 or more} \texttt{Vigor} through combat damage -- take a \texttt{Lethal Injury}.
    \item[\ding{170}] If you lost half or more of your remaining \texttt{Vigor}, but not all of it - take a \texttt{Serious Injury} or fall unconscious
    \item[\textbf{\textdagger}] If you lost all your \texttt{Vigor} or more through combat damage - take a \texttt{Lethal Injury}
\end{itemize}

If more than one condition applies, take the worst one. You should never take more than one \texttt{Injury} at a time. 
Check for each \texttt{Attack} separately. 

\subsection*{Consequences of an Injury.} When knight suffers an \texttt{Injury} it fills corresponding injury slot, recording a die that caused an injury. 
If a knight is out of those slots, fill a one with next lethality. If they are out of those, the knight is \textbf{Slain}. 

Each injury comes with consequences, after filling a slot roll \texttt{d4} and consult the table below.

\texttt{Trauma} counts as a \texttt{Serious Injury} and can potentially cause a heart attack and kill a knight if they are out of \texttt{Injury slots}.

\begin{multicols*}{2}

\subsection*{Breather}

\paragraph{Guard} requires only a swift moment of safety to recover.

\paragraph{Stress \& Focus} can also be restored quickly, but require consuming certain items.

\begin{itemize}[nosep]
    \item \textbf{Stimulant} for \texttt{Focus}
    \item \textbf{Sedative} for \texttt{Stress}
\end{itemize}
If knight has no \texttt{Focus} left, they can recover \texttt{1} during a \texttt{breather} by acquiring one \texttt{Stress}.

\subsection*{Short Rest}

In order to recover spent \texttt{Focus} and reduce acquired \texttt{Stress} at least an hour of rest is needed. 

It is not enough time to make a fire and cook food, but just right to treat injuries, have a talk or just zone out to process latest events.

\paragraph*{Focus.} This time is effective in restoring \texttt{Focus}, but compounding weariness of the day will make itself noticed.
Each time knight does an hour of \texttt{Short Rest} they restore \texttt{Focus} up to their maximum minus number of \texttt{Short Rests} they already had, including this one.
The first \texttt{Short Rest} of the day restores maximum, but one \texttt{Focus}. The second -- maximum, but two and so on. 
If it was a long day \texttt{Short Rest} does not provide any relief, but can never reduce \texttt{Focus}.

\paragraph{Injuries} can be treated during \texttt{Short Rest} without rolls. Knights have basic medical skills to dress and clean wounds. Mark all \texttt{Injuries} as \texttt{Treated}.

\paragraph{Stress} restores differently, each time knights rest for at least an hour they reduce their \texttt{Stress} by half. 

% Maybe spend one Focus to reduce half of the stress?
% Maybe exactly like Focus, but reduction?

\paragraph{Virture restoration}. During \texttt{Short Rest} it is possible to restore Virture, provided a knight has required supplies. 
\begin{itemize}[nosep]
    \item \textbf{Sustenance} for \texttt{Vigor}
    \item \textbf{Tonic} for \texttt{Clarity}
    \item \textbf{Sacrament} for \texttt{Spirit}
\end{itemize} They are consumed upon usage and provide individual restoration by themselves, but can be shared with Company during \texttt{Short Rest}.

If no supplies are used, a knight with \texttt{0} in some \texttt{Virtue} can restore it to \texttt{1}.

\subsection*{Long Rest}

\paragraph{Virture restoration.}

Long rest in warm hospitality with healthy meal removes \texttt{Stress} and restores \texttt{Virtues} and \texttt{Focus}.

\paragraph{Healing an Injury} removes it completely. This is a tricky endeavour which usually requires professional to accomplish and comes with a price tag. 

Still, in dire circumstances knight can attempt to heal themselves or other knight: 
\begin{enumerate}[nosep, leftmargin=*]
    \item Roll a single \texttt{d20} against healing knight's \texttt{Clarity}, below or equal is a success. No \texttt{Focus} or \texttt{Stress} can be used, as knight has no mastery over craft of healing.
    \item Roll a single \texttt{d20} against healed knight's \texttt{Vigor}, below or equal is a success. No \texttt{Focus} or \texttt{Stress} can be used.
\end{enumerate}

Only if both of the above rolls are successful, the knight is healed from an \texttt{Injury}. 

\paragraph{Getting tougher and acquiring quirks.} After suffering through agony of medicine without painkillers a knight is relieved from an injury completely. 

If total \texttt{Guard} of a knight is less than maximum value on a die that is marked on the \texttt{Injury Slot} roll it and gain that much more.

Choose or make Referee choose a Quirk for you that is described below and permanently add it to your knight. 


\paragraph{Coping with Trauma.} Referee decides how long it would persist and how it should be treated. By default, on the first morning after having a \texttt{Trauma} it heals itself, but leaves a permanent mark. 
Acquire a \texttt{Quirk} as if a knight healed from an \texttt{Injury}. 

\end{multicols*}

\section*{\centering \fontsize{35}{45}\selectfont Quirks}
\vspace*{0.4 cm}

\begin{multicols*}{2}

\paragraph{Bloodlust.} A visceral reaction to blood fuels strength, but lessens control. 
You can \texttt{Upface} one die of your weapon if you are wounded. However, if somebody is wounded, you can't engage them with nonlethal force

\paragraph{Desensitized.} The things you saw made everything else bleak in compresent, you don't feel much ever since.
When you treat an injury on the battlefield you roll with an additional \texttt{Focus}. However, you can't treat yourself.

\paragraph{Thickskinned.} After you recovered, your skin changed. It became rougher and harder to pierce.
You ignore first damage to your \texttt{Vigor} per combat if it is not larger than \texttt{2}. 
However, when your injury is treated on the battlefield the roll is treated as if it is opposed with an additional \texttt{Focus}. 

\paragraph{High pain tolerance.} Your body seems determine to never let you suffer again. 
You can ignore up to one \texttt{Agony} consequence of your \texttt{injury}. 
However, each time you roll 20 on a \texttt{Vigor} check you lose \texttt{1} \texttt{Vigor}.

\paragraph{Masocistic.} Pain is unbearable, but somehow it's a good thing. You regain two \texttt{Focus} each time you receive a serious injury that is not from \texttt{Trauma}. 
You regain all \texttt{Focus} if you receive a \texttt{Lethal Injury}. However, you can't reduce stress during \texttt{Short Rest}.

\paragraph{Blade obsession.} At the beginning of each day, randomly determine a weapon you own that you are obsessed with. 
You can \texttt{Upface} any die of that weapon. However, each time you strike, you must strike with the weapon you are obsessed with. 
You can't part with it, during combat it must be in your hand, otherwise at arms length at most.

\paragraph{Gore facination.} You ignore \texttt{Distress} conditions of your \texttt{Injuries}. However, you must treat your \texttt{Injuries} by yourself. 

\paragraph{Stressjuncky.} Each time you gain \texttt{Stress}, also gain \texttt{Focus}. 
However, your maximum \texttt{Stress} is halved, rounded down. It can't go lower than \texttt{1}.

\paragraph{Sadistic.} Restore one \texttt{Focus} each time an \texttt{Attack} you participate in reduces both \texttt{Vigor} and \texttt{Guard}. 
However, gain one \texttt{Stress} when an attack you participate in does not reduce \texttt{Vigor}. If this point of \texttt{Stress} would cause \texttt{Trauma} gain no \texttt{Stress} instead.

\paragraph{Smile like no other.} You don't smile a lot, but when you do, people feel what you have been through. 
You can \texttt{Downface} any die which use in Attack against you. 
However, whenever you take \texttt{Clarity} save during socialization treat it if it was opposed by one additional \texttt{Focus}.



\end{multicols*}

Spellcasting resources is represented by a set of d6 dice equal to the value of \texttt{Clarity}. Each time you restore Spirit via indulging a \texttt{Passion}, you can regain up to two missing dice from this set.

To cast a spell, a caster rolls any number of dice as an Action from that set and then decides what to cast. Only one spell can be cast per roll. If the spell does not state otherwise, Smite feat is not applicable for attacks performed within them.

\subsection*{Detect Magic}

Pick any number of dice, for each one which rolled for a maximum value you can ask a question about surrounding magic and receive truthful answer, but the amount of words in that answer can't exceed the value rolled on the die. For each other die, the answer is either yes, no or silence if the question is not applicable. Information for the answer is provided by spells around you and yourself. For example, spells usually are not directly marked by their caster and are not aware of it's surroundings beyond the spells design.

\subsection*{Recuperation of Magic}

When caster rolls dice to cast a spell, additionally to casting they can choose half, rounded down, of remaining dice and discard the rest. 
These chosen dice can be stored to cast spells later as if you rolled them. They persist until spent, but the total amount of dice(stored and unrolled) can't exceed the value of \texttt{Clarity}.
